Experiment 17 · History features and non-wildfire flag

Does longer memory, and separating wildfires from other burning, help the model?

Purpose: Experiment 16 showed that most "quiet" fires are either burning with no BC Wildfire Service fire on record (recurring, small, often at night) or older wildfires flaring up beyond the 7-day, one-cell history window. This experiment adds 30-day, season and prior-year history, and a wildfire-linked target.

Six Fire Centres · next-day forecasts · train 2018–2021 · thresholds 2022 · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

Setup

  • History features (12): detections in the cell and its neighbours over 30 days; active cells within 5 rings over 30 days; distance to the nearest detection in 30 days; days with activity since May 1; recent days with and without a known BCWS fire nearby; and, from earlier seasons, how many years the cell and its neighbours burned and how many of those detection days were or were not wildfire-linked.
  • Wildfire-linked target: a detection counts if it falls inside, or within 2 km of, the final perimeter of a BCWS fire active within 3 days, or within 10 km of an active BCWS fire's ignition point. Final perimeters are known only after the season, so they define labels and prior-year features only; current-season features use the ignition-point link with incidents already reported.
  • Models: CatBoost with Experiment 13's features (the previous best), with the history features added, and the latter retrained on the wildfire target. All use Experiment 03's sampling and correction.

Conclusion

  • History features did not help. Median F1 changed by -0.004 over Experiment 13 features on the recorded target (one-cell margin), and exact-cell AP fell slightly. The best configuration is still Experiment 13's CatBoost.
  • Quiet fires stay below the alert threshold. At thresholds chosen for F1, the models flag at most 0.6% of quiet fires in any group. History features raise quiet cells' scores (quiet positives average far more nearby 30-day activity than quiet negatives), but a quiet cell's chance of fire tomorrow stays well under the threshold that maximizes F1, so they are never flagged.
  • Wildfire-only target: 95% of training positives are wildfire-linked, and in 2023 nearly all are, so the cleaner target changes little in 2023 (+0.009 median F1 against the rule). It matters in quiet years and in the Peace region, where non-wildfire burning dominates.
  • Filtering yesterday's detections by known BCWS fires hurts (median F1 well below the plain rule): many real fire detections are not yet within 10 km of a reported ignition point.
  • Next: land-use, cutblock, industrial-site, fuel and terrain layers (being built in datasets/static_layers/) give the model direct evidence about non-wildfire burning and ignition risk; quiet cells may also need their own model and operating point instead of one F1 threshold for all cells.
  • The wildfire link depends on BCWS records; small wildfires never reported to BCWS are counted as non-wildfire.
  • 30-day windows are truncated at May 1, since detections cover May–September only. Prior-year features start in 2018, so 2018 rows have none.
  • Untuned CatBoost, one seed. 2024 remains excluded.
