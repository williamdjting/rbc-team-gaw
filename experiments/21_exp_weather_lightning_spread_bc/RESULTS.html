Experiment 21 · Weather, lightning and spread inputs

Can drivers the rule cannot see beat persistence?

Purpose: on the two-satellite label, "fire yesterday" misses fire spreading into new cells and new starts, and keeps flagging fires that stop. This experiment gives the model weather, lightning and the wind direction relative to neighbouring fires, and checks whether it now beats the two-satellite rule.

6 Fire Centres · train 2018–2021 · thresholds 2022 · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

Setup

  • Label and training: as Experiment 20: S-NPP or NOAA-20 detection in the cell, gap-filled; 2018–2020 train on gap-filled S-NPP until FIRMS NOAA-20 arrives. NOAA-20 inputs are blank before 2021.
  • Weather: BC Wildfire Service station values at noon local standard time on the day before the target (20:00 UTC, known before that UTC day ends): temperature, humidity, wind, 24 h and 3-day rain, Fire Weather Index codes. Each cell takes its nearest station with a reading, among its three nearest within 100 km.
  • Lightning: CanCPLD cloud-to-ground flashes (0.1°) on that UTC day and the two before: the cell, 3×3 and 9×9 blocks, and days since lightning nearby.
  • Spread: burning neighbours one and two cells away, each weighted by the cosine of the angle between its direction and the direction the wind blows from (positive = fire upwind), and that sum times wind speed.
  • Thresholds: tuned to maximise 2022 F1 per Fire Centre, or with no tuning: each day, flag as many cells as the two-satellite rule does (the model only chooses which ones).

Conclusion

  • For the first time, a model beats the two-satellite rule with an interval above zero, but only with the one-cell margin. The model with weather, lightning and spread inputs and no day_of_year reaches BC-wide F1 0.824 against 0.810: +0.015 (95% interval +0.005 to +0.025). It is ahead in 6 of 6 centres (2 with intervals above zero), and 6 of 6 centres reach 0.70.
  • On the exact cell the rule still wins: 0.607 against 0.624, -0.017 (95% interval -0.029 to -0.008). The model is within 0.01 of the rule in four centres (intervals include zero) and leads in Southeast, but trails by 0.023 in Prince George, which holds 72% of 2023's fire cells.
  • Dropping day_of_year mattered more than any new input. With it, the same inputs score 0.585 / 0.791; without it, 0.607 / 0.824. Weather and lightning added to a model that keeps day_of_year do not help (0.575 / 0.811). With the calendar available, the model learns when 2018 and 2021 peaked; without it, it has to use the weather, which is now its largest input group (36% of importance; noon temperature alone 17%). Lightning (2.0%) and the upwind inputs (0.6%) contribute little.
  • The win comes from how many cells are flagged, not from better choices among the rule's cells. At the rule's daily volume, the same model scores 0.606 / 0.807, a tie with the margin and behind on the exact cell; the NOAA-20 model without weather scores about the same (0.603 / 0.807). With its 2022 threshold and the margin, the model flags 80,473 cells against the rule's 55,122: extra flags around active fires, which the margin counts as correct when fire appears next door. Its scores also carry 2022 thresholds over to 2023 better than the calendar models.
  • On the exact cell, its errors shift but don't shrink enough. It drops 5,397 of the rule's 19,569 false alarms on fires that stop and catches 2,058 of 23,361 new fire cells, but loses 3,928 still-burning cells and adds 4,271 other false alarms.
  • Selection check: this model was picked as the best of four on 2023, which flatters it. It was also the best model in 2022 (0.598 exact, 0.765 margin, medians), so the choice would have been the same without looking at 2023. A clean confirmation still needs 2024.
  • Next: keep day_of_year out. Rerun with FIRMS NOAA-20 for 2018–2020 when it arrives. Try gridded weather (ERA5-Land): 15–24% of Coastal and Northwest cell-days have no station within 100 km, and a station's wind is a weak guide to where a fire will spread. Weather forecasts for the target day are the operational version of the missing information.
  • Training still uses the stopgap label: 2018–2020 lack NOAA-20 in both inputs and labels.
  • Station weather is a point reading up to 100 km away, at noon of the day before the target; the target day's own weather (rain, wind shifts) is unknown to the model. Weather forecasts would be the operational substitute.
  • 2023 has been used to compare many experiments; only 2024 will give an untouched number. Untuned CatBoost, one seed. Neighbourhoods stop at each Fire Centre's grid edge.
