Experiment 15 · Forecast lead time

Does the model pull away from persistence at longer leads?

Purpose: at a 1-day lead the previous-day rule is hard to beat. Its F1 falls quickly with lead time, and most fires at longer leads appear in cells that were not burning on day 0. This experiment trains one CatBoost model per lead (1, 2, 3, 5, 7 days) on Experiment 13's features and compares it with the best of six persistence rules.

Six Fire Centres · train 2018–2021 · rules and thresholds chosen on 2022 per centre, lead and definition · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

Conclusion

  • The model's lead over persistence stays small at every lead. With the one-cell margin it is a median +0.020 at 2 days and +0.021 at 3 days. Even with the best threshold in hindsight it is +0.003 at 5 days and +0.004 at 7 days.
  • Fire-history inputs carry little beyond persistence at any lead. The fires the rules miss at longer leads start in quiet cells, and nothing in the current inputs says where. Predicting them needs drivers the model does not yet have: weather for the forecast days, fuels and terrain, and lightning.
  • At 5 and 7 days, 2022 thresholds are unreliable. 2022 had almost no fire at long leads in several centres, so some 2023 results drop well below what the model can do (for example Cariboo at 5 days). Pooled-year thresholds are needed before long leads are compared again.
  • Targets are recorded detections on day e + L. Margin scoring follows Experiment 14.
  • Untuned CatBoost, one seed, one model per lead. 2024 remains excluded.
