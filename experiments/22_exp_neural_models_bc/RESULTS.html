Experiment 22 · Deep learning vs CatBoost

Does a deep learning model learn more than CatBoost?

Purpose: every model so far has been gradient-boosted trees on hand-built features. This experiment trains an MLP on the same inputs, and an MLP plus a GRU that reads the last 14 days of each cell's fire, weather, lightning and spread history day by day, to see whether either learns something CatBoost misses.

6 Fire Centres · train 2018–2021 · thresholds 2022 · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

Setup

  • Same everything except the model: Experiment 21's label, training draw, inputs (weather, lightning and upwind spread, no day_of_year), thresholds and scoring. Experiment 21's CatBoost is loaded, not retrained, as the reference to beat; it reproduces its numbers exactly.
  • MLP: three hidden layers (256, 256, 128). Heavy-tailed inputs get a signed log; all inputs are standardized; blanks (NOAA-20 before 2021, no station within 100 km) become 0 with a missing flag.
  • MLP + GRU: the same MLP, plus a 64-unit GRU over the 14 days ending on the day before the target. Each day has 25 channels per cell: S-NPP detections in the cell and rings 1–2, FRP, night detections, NOAA-20 and two-satellite detections nearby, station weather, lightning, upwind fire and an in-season flag.
  • Training: binary cross-entropy with AdamW, the same sampled rows and quiet-row correction as CatBoost. One 7-day block in six of each training season is held out to pick the epoch; the neural models therefore train on about 5/6 of the rows CatBoost uses.

Conclusion

Partly. Both networks rank cells better than CatBoost and win on the exact cell. With the one-cell margin, CatBoost keeps a small lead.

  • Exact cell: the MLP scores 0.626 BC-wide. CatBoost scores 0.607, so the MLP is +0.019 (95% interval +0.011 to +0.027) ahead of it. The MLP+GRU scores 0.623, +0.016 (95% interval +0.007 to +0.025) ahead of CatBoost. Both gains hold at the rule's daily volume too (MLP +0.013 (95% interval +0.008 to +0.017)). The MLP beats CatBoost in 4 of 6 centres. Median average precision, which needs no threshold, rises from 0.617 to 0.642 (MLP) and 0.642 (MLP+GRU).
  • But the exact cell is still a tie with the rule: the MLP minus the two-satellite rule (0.624) is +0.002 (95% interval -0.003 to +0.007). At the rule's volume it is -0.004. This is the first learned model to reach the rule on the exact cell, though not to beat it.
  • One-cell margin: CatBoost stays ahead. MLP minus CatBoost is -0.004 (95% interval -0.011 to +0.003); MLP+GRU minus CatBoost is -0.008 (95% interval -0.016 to -0.001). Both networks still beat the rule (MLP +0.010 (95% interval +0.004 to +0.018)). At the rule's volume the networks edge CatBoost (MLP +0.004 (95% interval +0.001 to +0.007)). The margin difference therefore comes from where each model's 2022 threshold lands, not from better ranking: CatBoost's threshold buys recall (0.844 vs 0.799), which the margin rewards.
  • Where the exact-cell gain comes from: the networks keep more cells that are still burning. The MLP keeps 33,955 and CatBoost 31,625, out of 35,553. They catch about as many new fire cells (2,200 vs 2,058 of 23,361). The networks do not find new fires; they are better at not dropping fires that continue.
  • The 14-day GRU adds nothing over the MLP. It catches more new fire cells (3,006) but pays for them in false alarms (6,455 vs 4,456). Its F1 and AP equal or trail the MLP's, and it takes about four times longer to fit. The hand-built rolling features already summarise the recent history.
  • Both networks peak after one epoch. Held-out loss rises afterwards while training loss keeps falling, so the networks overfit the training seasons almost at once. Lower learning rates, stronger regularisation or averaging several seeds are the obvious next steps. The current numbers are a lower bound on what a network can do here.

The deep learning model is a modest step: it closes the exact-cell gap to the rule without opening a new one, and all models stay within about 0.02 F1 of each other. With the margin every model is above the 0.70–0.80 goal (0.816 to 0.824); the exact cell stays near 0.63. Neither result moves the information ceiling seen since Experiment 15. FIRMS NOAA-20 for 2018–2020 and gridded day-ahead weather remain the levers most likely to.

  • Training still uses the stopgap label: 2018–2020 lack NOAA-20 in both inputs and labels.
  • One seed and one architecture per neural model, lightly tuned (no search over width, depth or learning rate); trained on CPU.
  • 2023 has been used to compare many experiments; only 2024 will give an untouched number. Neighbourhoods stop at each Fire Centre's grid edge.
