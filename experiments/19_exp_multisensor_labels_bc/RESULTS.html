Experiment 19 · Multi-satellite labels

Can more satellites make the next-day fire label more consistent?

Purpose: the current label is one satellite's detection (VIIRS on S-NPP). A burning cell often goes undetected for a day because of cloud, smoke or the overpass, which caps every forecast. This experiment builds labels from more satellites and from filling one-day gaps, and measures how consistent each one is.

Six Fire Centres · target days May 8 – September 30 · NOAA-20 complete 2021–2023 · 2024 excluded

Data

  • CWFIS hotspot archive (Natural Resources Canada, open): VIIRS on S-NPP and NOAA-20, MODIS on Terra and Aqua, and AVHRR, merged for North America. Kept: BC, May–September 2018–2023, assigned to H3 res-7 cells. NOAA-20 is complete from 2021, partial in 2019 (from July) and 2020, and absent in 2018. AVHRR (2018–2020 only) and VIIRS M-band are left out so the sensor mix stays stable.
  • Gap filling: a cell-day also counts as positive if the cell is positive the day before and the day after. This uses the next day, so it is for labels only. The rule and the quiet test always use the unfilled label, which is all a forecast can see.
  • Measures: P(fire today | fire yesterday); the missed-day share (a cell positive within 3 days before and after, but not that day); the share of positives in quiet cells (nothing in the cell or its neighbours for 7 days); the previous-day rule's F1; and the share of positives inside, or within 2 km of, a final BCWS fire perimeter of that year.
  • Alignment check: CWFIS's own copy of S-NPP matches FIRMS best with no date shift (overlap 0.51–0.92 at lag 0 vs at most 0.44 at ±1 day), so dates line up.

In quiet years CWFIS keeps fewer S-NPP detections than FIRMS, and more of them fall inside BCWS perimeters (2019: 48% vs 31%, 2020: 81% vs 53%, 2022: 97% vs 90%). CWFIS appears to drop some industrial and other non-wildfire hotspots.

Conclusion

  • Adding NOAA-20 makes the label clearly more consistent. In 2023 it adds 19% more positive cell-days, nearly all inside BCWS perimeters (98.7%), and raises P(fire today | fire yesterday) from 0.536 to 0.576.
  • Gap filling adds the most. With both, the missed-day share falls from 40% to 27%. The rule's F1 on the exact cell rises from 0.536 to 0.624 in 2023, and from 0.525 to 0.614 in 2022.
  • MODIS does not help. Its 1 km pixels spread detections over neighbouring cells: it adds positives but not consistency, and slightly fewer fall inside perimeters.
  • CWFIS's copy cleans quiet years. Using CWFIS for both VIIRS satellites gives the cleanest label in 2022 (97.5% inside perimeters vs 91.8%) at the cost of 14% of FIRMS S-NPP positives.
  • Correction: an earlier estimate of the rule on gap-filled labels (0.815, one-cell margin, 2023) let the rule see the filled day before, which uses the target day. Scored correctly, it is 0.780.
  • Next: retrain the forecast on the S-NPP + NOAA-20, gap-filled label and test whether the model now beats the rule. Training years need NOAA-20 too: CWFIS has it only from 2021 (partial 2019–2020), so the FIRMS NOAA-20 request (2018–2020) completes the training label.
  • A higher rule F1 means a more consistent, more predictable label. It does not by itself mean a better forecast of the current label; both are measured against their own label.
  • "Inside BCWS perimeter" uses final perimeters of the same year with a 2 km margin and no date window.
  • Neighbourhoods stop at each Fire Centre's grid edge. 2024 remains excluded.
