Experiment 13 · Fire-context features

Do richer fire-context features raise F1?

Purpose: the models in Experiments 03–07 all reach F1 of about 0.45–0.58, close to what their inputs allow. This experiment adds new information instead of a new model: fire intensity, a wider neighbourhood, fire-complex size and duration, and BC Wildfire Service incidents.

Six Fire Centres · same rows, target, split, sampling and correction as Experiment 03 · train 2018–2021 · thresholds 2022 · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

05 / Is the remaining gap to the rule real?

Two checks on the 2023 predictions. Noise: the 95% intervals resample whole calendar weeks of 2023 (2,000 draws), because fires span many days and single days are not independent. Threshold or scores: "rule volume" flags, each day, as many cells as the rule does (the number detected yesterday, known in advance), but picks them by model score. If the model ranked active cells better than "detected yesterday", this would beat the rule.

  • The gap closed. Base boosting was clearly below the rule in 5 of 6 centres (interval entirely below zero). For CatBoost, all fire-context features, 0 of 6 intervals exclude zero: the remaining differences are within noise.
  • But nothing beats the rule at its own operating point. Even the best threshold chosen on 2023 in hindsight is a median +0.001 from the rule, and at the rule's alert volume the model is a median +0.003 (0 of 6 intervals exclude zero). Given the same number of alerts, the model picks cells about as well as "detected yesterday".
  • So the previous-day rule sits on the best precision–recall curve these inputs allow, near its F1 peak. The AP gains are real, but they are away from that point: fewer, more confident alerts, or more recall at lower precision. To raise F1 itself, the model needs information on which burning cells stop the next day (day-t rain and wind), and labels with fewer missed days (NOAA-20).

08 / Conclusion

  • The new inputs are the largest ranking gain so far: all four groups change boosting AP by a median of +0.045 and F1 by +0.013 over the identical base model. FRP and day/night carries most of the AP gain.
  • F1 now ties the previous-day rule but does not beat it: the best configuration (CatBoost, all fire-context features) is -0.004 median F1 from the rule, with 0 of 6 differences outside week-resampled noise. Base boosting was clearly below the rule in 5 of 6.
  • The ceiling is the inputs, not the threshold: at the rule's own alert volume, or at the best threshold in hindsight, the model only matches the rule. Beating it needs information about which active cells stop: day-t rain and wind (ERA5-Land; download script ready) and NOAA-20 labels (requested). This pipeline reruns unchanged on both.
  • Targets are recorded detections, not ignitions, unique fires or confirmed absence. Coverage remains unknown.
  • BCWS incidents: only records typed "Fire"; a missing out date is treated as active for 30 days and activity is capped at 180 days. Ignition dates may still be set after the fact for some fires; the one-day delay reduces but cannot rule out that leak.
  • Untuned models and a single seed: differences under about 0.01 F1 are within noise. 2023 is a development evaluation; 2024 remains excluded.
