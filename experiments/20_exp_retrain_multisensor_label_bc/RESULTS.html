Experiment 20 · Retraining on the multi-satellite label

With a cleaner label, does the model beat the rule?

Purpose: Experiment 19 built a more consistent label from both VIIRS satellites (S-NPP and NOAA-20) with one-day gap filling. This experiment retrains the forecast on it, using a stopgap for training years without NOAA-20.

6 Fire Centres · train 2018–2021 · thresholds 2022 · evaluate 2023 · 2024 excluded

Setup

  • Label: an S-NPP (FIRMS) or NOAA-20 (CWFIS) VIIRS detection in the cell, plus a cell positive the day before and the day after. Every forecast is scored on this label.
  • Stopgap training label: 2021 has the full label; 2018–2020 use gap-filled S-NPP only (NOAA-20 is absent in 2018 and partial in 2019–2020). FIRMS NOAA-20 for 2018–2020 will replace this.
  • Inputs: Experiment 13's features from S-NPP detections. One model adds NOAA-20 and two-satellite inputs (detections in the cell and its neighbours over 1, 3 and 7 days). No input uses gap filling.
  • Rules: the old rule (S-NPP detection yesterday), and the new bar, an S-NPP or NOAA-20 detection yesterday.

Conclusion

  • The two-satellite rule is the best forecast of the new label. BC-wide F1 0.624 on the exact cell and 0.810 with the one-cell margin; 6 of 6 centres reach 0.70 with the margin. It beats the old S-NPP rule by 0.039 (exact) and every model; all bootstrap intervals against it lie below 0.
  • Retraining on the stopgap label did not help: 0.582 / 0.790 BC-wide, the same as Experiment 13's model on the old label (0.582 / 0.795). Three of its four training years have no NOAA-20, so it mostly learns the old label, from S-NPP inputs only.
  • NOAA-20 inputs help, but one training season is not enough. Among the 2021-only models, NOAA-20 inputs add +0.012 (exact) and +0.016 (margin). In 2022, where its thresholds were tuned, that model matches or beats the two-satellite rule with the margin in 6 of 6 centres, but it falls behind in 2023: what it learned from one season does not carry over.
  • Why the rule wins: the cleaner label makes "burning yesterday, burning today" more reliable, and the two-satellite rule uses that directly. The models see NOAA-20 in at most one training season, so they cannot learn how much to trust it.
  • Next: rerun when FIRMS NOAA-20 for 2018–2020 arrives, so every training year has the full label and two-satellite inputs. Fire history alone has not beaten persistence in any experiment, so the next inputs should be drivers the rule cannot see: BCWS station weather (now downloaded) and lightning.
  • The training label is a stopgap: 2018–2020 lack NOAA-20, so models trained on those years learn a less consistent label than the one they are scored on.
  • Models trained on 2021 alone see one fire season, so their thresholds and scores may not transfer to other seasons.
  • Neighbourhoods stop at each Fire Centre's grid edge. Untuned CatBoost, one seed. 2024 remains excluded.
