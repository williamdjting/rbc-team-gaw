Class-imbalance technique: step by step

Selective random undersampling, followed by a sampling correction to scores. Applied in Experiments 03–04; not in persistence Experiments 01–02.

  1. Select training rows: keep every positive and every active row. Give each quiet negative a 1% chance of being retained. This is not sampling 1% of all negatives or making a 50/50 dataset.
  2. Train without class weights: Experiment 03 uses the saved sampled rows. Experiment 04 keeps the full graph, but uses a fresh quiet-negative sample in the training loss each step.
  3. Correct quiet-row scores: sampling makes positives appear more common among quiet examples. At prediction time, multiply predicted odds by 0.01 for every quiet row; active-row scores are unchanged. This uses past-history quiet status, never the unknown target label.
  4. Evaluate on all rows: apply the correction before selecting thresholds on 2022 and evaluating on 2023. Evaluation data is not undersampled.

Why correct the scores? An illustrative example

  • Suppose the original quiet training group has 100 positives and 10,000 negatives: its positive rate is 100 / 10,100 ≈ 0.99%.
  • Keeping all 100 positives and approximately 1% of negatives leaves 100 positives and about 100 negatives: roughly 50% positive in the sample.
  • The population did not change; sampling changed what the model saw. The correction accounts for this difference.

For an illustrative quiet-row prediction of 50%:

Original odds      = 0.50 / (1 - 0.50) = 1
Corrected odds     = 1 × 0.01 = 0.01
Corrected probability = 0.01 / (1 + 0.01) ≈ 0.99%

Code equivalent: corrected log-odds = model log-odds + log(0.01)

0.01 comes from the 1% negative-retention probability. It is not a buffer, clipping threshold, or training weight. This corrects the sampling-induced odds shift; it does not guarantee calibrated probabilities from a fitted model. No separate probability-calibration procedure has been fitted.

  • Positive means at least one recorded hotspot detection. A zero target means no recorded detection, not verified absence of fire; observation coverage remains unknown.
